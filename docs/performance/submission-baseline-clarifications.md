## 1. Scope & Purpose

This document provides rigorous methodological clarifications and scientific boundaries for the empirical results reported in the NetGuard v1.1 evaluation artifacts (`docs/evaluation/unseen-capture/`). It addresses terminology precision, sampling scope, telemetry boundaries, and architectural nuances to ensure transparent and defensible academic citation.

## 2. Telemetry and Evaluation Clarifications

### 2.1 Zeek Ground-Truth Corroboration Scope
- **Assessed vs. Total Alerts:** The reported "14/14 semantic corroborations" applies strictly to the 14 NetGuard alerts that fell within the spatial and temporal window of the publisher's labeled Zeek connection log (`CTU-IoT-Malware-1-1.conn.log.labeled`, containing 7,008 flows).
- **Unmatched Alert Ledger:** Exactly 1,846 NetGuard alerts remain unmatched by the available Zeek labeled sample because the published labeled subset covers only a discrete initial fraction of the total 4.66-day physical capture. NetGuard alerts outside this sample window cannot be evaluated against Zeek ground truth without complete dataset re-labeling.

### 2.2 Transport-Layer SYN Signals vs. Endpoint State
- **Authentication Service Brute-Force (`BRUTE_FORCE`):** NetGuard's `BruteForceRule` counts rapid TCP SYN bursts directed at designated authentication service ports (e.g. port 23 Telnet, port 22 SSH). A network-layer SYN-rate signal detects connection attempt density; it does **not** establish that credentials were submitted, decrypted, processed, or rejected at the endpoint application layer.
- **Volumetric Flooding (`SYN_FLOOD`):** NetGuard's `SynFloodRule` measures incoming TCP SYN frame rates exceeding configured thresholds (>100 pkts/10s). SYN-count detections identify abnormal ingress packet rates; they do **not** independently prove operating system TCP backlog queue saturation, socket starvation, or completed denial of service on the victim endpoint.

### 2.3 Memory Footprint & Asymptotic Bounds
- **Empirical Peak RSS vs. Theoretical $O(1)$ Guarantee:** The observed peak Resident Set Size of **110.61 MB** across the 1,686,291-packet replay is an **empirical hardware measurement** on this specific 4.66-day capture. While individual state containers enforce $O(1)$ bounded eviction via `OrderedDict` and `prune_stale(now)`, real-world memory footprint is also governed by Python runtime allocator fragmentation, internal interpreter heap overhead, and temporary OS file buffers. It must not be cited as a universal formal guarantee of constant physical memory across all conceivable adversarial workloads.

### 2.4 Time Horizon Terminology
- **Capture Duration vs. Detection Window:** The evaluated PCAP spans **402,852.02 seconds (~4.66 days)** of continuous capture. This physical duration represents the real-world temporal span of the dataset and is mathematically distinct from the detection rules' sliding-window intervals (e.g., 10 seconds for `PortScanRule` and `SynFloodRule`, 120 seconds for `SlowPortScanRule`, 300 seconds for `IrcC2BeaconingRule`).

### 2.5 False-Positive Characterization on Selected Traffic
- **Benign Protocol Suppression:** NetGuard produced 0 false alarms on the 415,000 UDP frames, 33,249 ARP broadcasts, 21,191 ICMP messages, and 15,356 NTP frames in this capture. While this confirms that NetGuard's rules correctly discriminate standard network protocols and do not alert indiscriminately on background noise, suppression on this specific capture **does not establish a general zero false-positive rate** across all benign enterprise or campus network topologies.
- **DOF P2P Traffic Context:** The 387,888 Decentralized Over-the-Air Forwarding (DOF) UDP frames present in the capture are generated by the custom P2P mesh protocol of the Hide & Seek botnet itself. NetGuard's suppression of alerts on these frames reflects an intentional scope boundary (NetGuard lacks a dedicated DOF P2P signature), not verification of standard benign internet traffic.

### 2.6 Suricata Cross-Engine Divergence Nuances
- **Configuration and Ruleset Specificity:** Suricata's absence of alerts on horizontal port 23 scans and SYN bursts on this capture is specific to the evaluated setup: Suricata 8.0.7 operating with the default Emerging Threats (ET) Open ruleset without custom `threshold.config` directives or specialized scan preprocessor tuning. Enterprise installations with custom rate thresholds or dedicated vertical scan preprocessors may exhibit different detection profiles. Suricata remains an industry-standard payload signature engine with complementary strengths to NetGuard's stateful behavioral heuristics.
